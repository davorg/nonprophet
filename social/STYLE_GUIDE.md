# Social copy style

Social posts are written for a general reader scrolling on a phone. Do not assume
that the reader knows the Bible, Christian theology, or our verdict system.

Each carousel has five slides: the claim in plain English; the Old Testament text;
the claimed New Testament fulfilment; why Christians connect the passages; and the
problem with calling the earlier passage a prediction.

## Language rules

- Use everyday English and short sentences.
- Give each slide one job and one main idea.
- Prefer names to ambiguous pronouns.
- Explain an idea instead of using a technical term.
- Describe the Christian argument fairly before criticising it.
- Separate “Christians later connected these stories” from “the earlier text
  predicted Jesus.”
- End with a conclusion a reader could repeat in ordinary conversation.
- Keep headings under 12 words and bodies under 65 words.
- Avoid unexplained specialist language, including *canonical*, *Christological*,
  *typology*, *referent*, *exegesis*, *retrospective rereading*, *predictive
  specificity*, and *bodily translation*.

The editorial record remains the source for research and website copy.
The file `social/plain-language.json` is the source for carousel wording.

## Captions and hashtags

Each claim has one platform-neutral suggested caption in `social/captions.json`.
Keep it under 220 characters so the caption and its five hashtags can also fit in
a Bluesky post. State the claim fairly, then give the conclusion in everyday
English. Do not merely repeat every slide or ask for engagement.

Each caption must have exactly five hashtags. The default set is `#Bible`,
`#Jesus`, `#Prophecy`, `#Christianity`, and `#NonProphet`; change one only when a
more specific tag is genuinely useful.
