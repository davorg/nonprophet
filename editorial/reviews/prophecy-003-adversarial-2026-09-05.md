## Findings

1. Calling Luke 24:51 and Acts 1:9 “independent” ascension narratives is
   source-critically inaccurate because they belong to the two-volume Lukan work.
   Rephrase them as additional Lukan witnesses.
2. The absence of an explicit NT identification of Enoch as a type should not be
   treated as a requirement for later Christian typology. Ecclesial or theological
   typology can be coherent without explicit NT authorization.
3. The inherited claim says “illustrated,” not “predicted” or “fulfilled.” Separate
   the judgments explicitly: as prediction, no; as typological illustration,
   plausible but limited.
4. Hebrews 11:5 removes much of Genesis's ambiguity within a Christian canonical
   reading by saying that Enoch was taken so that he did not see death. Give this
   positive evidence its full weight.
5. Elijah, Psalm 68 and Daniel 7 are clearer biblical comparators for ascension or
   exaltation. They place Enoch in a broader ascent motif but also reduce its
   specificity.
6. Hebrews's no-death interpretation can support bodily translation. Say that a
   bodily ascent is not explicit in Genesis, rather than suggesting it is impossible.
7. Narrow “no cited NT author” to the relevant observation: no cited NT text presents
   Jesus' ascension as fulfilment of Enoch's translation.

No major straw man or clear textual/citation error was found. The qualification about
Mark's Longer Ending was judged fair and appropriately balanced by the cited minority
defence.

## Strongest rebuttal

Within a Christian canonical reading, Genesis 5:24 is not left alone: Hebrews 11:5
authoritatively interprets Enoch as taken by God so that he did not see death. Jesus
is later taken into heaven and exalted at God's right hand. Typology does not require
Genesis to name Jesus, predict a future event or contain every later doctrinal detail.
On that standard, Enoch can fairly be called an early biblical illustration of bodily
translation into God's presence, even though it remains weak as prediction and less
direct than Elijah, Psalm 68 or Daniel 7.
