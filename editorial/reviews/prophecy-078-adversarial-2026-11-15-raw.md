The packet supports a close textual relationship and a retrospective Christian application. It does **not** establish that literary dependence excludes historical occurrence or theological fulfilment. The principal weakness is the movement from an observable correspondence to stronger conclusions about how Matthew composed the scene and what the psalm cannot mean.

1. **The strongest Christian case establishes correspondence but barely argues for fulfilment.**

   The supplied “strongest case” largely repeats what the critical case accepts: Matthew deliberately connects Jesus with the psalm’s sufferer. It omits the stronger argument that a lament can acquire a fuller messianic application, or that historically occurring events can instantiate a scriptural pattern which an evangelist then makes explicit.

   Calling the alternative “an independent forecast fulfilled by chance” also misframes the disagreement. Christian fulfilment need not mean either independent wording or chance fulfilment.

   **Material effect:** Yes, for the fairness and scope of the verdict. This objection does not establish prediction, but it undermines a dismissal of fulfilment generally.

   **Evidence needed:** A representative Christian argument explaining whether the claim concerns direct prediction, typology, fuller divine intention, or some combination. The packet’s narrow “predicted … taunting” claim should remain distinguishable from those broader claims.

2. **Literary dependence does not establish that Matthew invented the reported taunt.**

   “Matthew knows the psalm and uses it to construct the scene” can imply that the event originated in his composition. The correspondence supports scriptural shaping of the narration; it cannot by itself distinguish invention from the selection or adaptation of historical speech, inherited tradition, or a historically scriptural taunt.

   The packet need not prove that the taunt occurred. It must avoid treating dependence as proof that it did not.

   **Material effect:** Yes, if “construct” carries a claim about historical origin. Less so if it refers only to narrative presentation. The conclusion that this wording supplies no *independent* corroboration is substantially narrower and more defensible.

   **Evidence needed:** Comparative passion accounts, evidence concerning Matthew’s sources, and the cited scholarship’s actual arguments about tradition and composition.

3. **“Present lament” does not alone settle every question about prophetic meaning.**

   The inference “the source is a present lament; therefore it does not predict a future repetition” needs additional support. A literary speaker’s present circumstances establish the immediate setting of the utterance, not necessarily the exhaustive limits of its canonical or theological significance.

   Conversely, nothing supplied positively establishes a future messianic prediction. The appropriate distinction is between an absent demonstrated prediction and a demonstrated impossibility of prophetic significance.

   **Material effect:** Potentially. It affects the categorical wording and confidence more than the classification of Matthew’s use as retrospective.

   **Evidence needed:** The full psalm, an argument from its genre and structure, and explicit criteria for what would count as prediction rather than later application.

4. **Original meaning, reception, and typology are treated as more mutually exclusive than they are.**

   “Retrospective rereading” describes an interpreter’s relationship to an earlier text. It does not independently determine whether that rereading is historically grounded, canonically warranted, or theologically true. A Christian interpreter could accept retrospective identification while also affirming typological fulfilment.

   Similarly, “explicit interpretation” should be distinguished from an explicit citation: the supplied Matthew verse contains no quotation formula identifying Psalm 22.

   **Material effect:** Yes, if the verdict intends to rule out fulfilment broadly; limited if it assesses only the narrowly stated advance-prediction claim.

   **Evidence needed:** Clear definitions of the verdict categories and an explanation of whether retrospective rereading can coexist with typological fulfilment.
