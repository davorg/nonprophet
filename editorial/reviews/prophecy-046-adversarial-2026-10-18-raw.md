2. **Immediate narrative meaning does not exclude additional typological significance**

   “Ruth describes what Boaz did … not what a future Messiah would do” treats those possibilities as mutually exclusive without establishing why. A narrative can recount an immediate event and subsequently receive a typological interpretation. Whether it also carries intended divine anticipation is a further theological question that this packet does not settle.

   The different problems addressed—property and family continuity versus forgiveness—establish that the passages are not describing identical transactions. Difference alone does not defeat typology, which can involve transformation or enlargement of an earlier pattern.

   **Material effect:** Yes, for the categorical exclusion of prediction or anticipation; less so for the narrower finding that prediction is unestablished.  
   **Evidence needed:** An explicit definition of prediction, the criteria separating analogy from prospective typology, and arguments for applying those criteria here.
