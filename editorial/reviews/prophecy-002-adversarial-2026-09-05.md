## Findings

   rereading or typology; canonical interpreters need not expect the referent to be

2. Repeated `shuph` does not make the blows equally severe: head/heel location may

3. Calling the serpent an ordinary animal is too flat: it speaks, deceives and opposes

4. Give Romans 16:20, Revelation 12:9 and 20:2, and Jewish reception their proper
   weight, while explaining what they can and cannot show about original meaning.
5. Separate the source claim's transformations: serpent to Satan, collective or
   ambiguous offspring to Jesus, and reciprocal striking to decisive defeat.
6. Absence of a quotation formula does not by itself rule out allusion; apply broader
   intertextual criteria to Hebrews 2:14 and 1 John 3:8.
7. Treat the LXX masculine pronoun as material to reception even if inconclusive for
   the Hebrew original, and phrase the aetiological reading without reductionism.

## Strongest rebuttal

Genesis need not name Satan or Jesus to be prophetic. It introduces a hostile serpent,
promises continuing conflict and depicts the woman's seed striking its head. Later
canonical texts identify the ancient serpent as the devil, describe Jesus destroying
the devil and his works, and echo Satan crushed under believers' feet. On this view,
the NT discloses the fuller referent of a deliberately seed-form promise rather than
inventing it.
