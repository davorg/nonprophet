The public verdict, **“Explicit elsewhere, unnamed here,” is well supported by the packet.** The stronger classification, “retrospective_rereading” with “high” confidence, needs qualification: confidence that John does not name Psalm 16 is different from confidence that its resurrection application is exclusively retrospective. No decisive factual error or inflammatory wording is established by the supplied material.

1. **Potential straw man: treating explicit prediction as the only meaningful fulfilment claim.**
   “Preservation from death is not resurrection after death” correctly distinguishes two events. It does not establish that preservation exhausts the psalm’s meaning, or answer a Christian account involving typology or a fuller divine intention. The record acknowledges Christian reception but does not adequately develop these alternatives.
   **Material effect:** Could change the categorical classification or its confidence; would not change John’s lack of an explicit Psalm 16 citation.
   **Evidence needed:** A stated criterion for fulfilment and a substantive account of the strongest Christian interpretation, including whether it claims direct prediction, typology, or both.

2. **Logical gap: Acts establishes Christian use, but not John’s particular referent.**
   Acts’s use makes Psalm 16 a plausible candidate. Calling it a “strong” or “leading” candidate for John requires additional evidence connecting John’s wording or argument to that passage, or comparing other plausible explanations. The singular “Scripture” does not, on the evidence supplied, settle whether John means one passage or a broader scriptural pattern.
   **Material effect:** Could weaken the proposed identification with John; it would leave the public verdict intact.
   **Evidence needed:** John’s immediate context, relevant linguistic analysis, and comparison of the proposed Psalm 16 connection with competing interpretations.

3. **Omitted material evidence: the actual argument in Acts.**
   The packet repeatedly says that Acts applies the psalm to Jesus, but does not reproduce the cited speeches or explain the premises of their “Davidic-resurrection” argument. Consequently, the strongest Christian evidence is reported rather than presented for assessment. The source descriptions likewise supply no substantive excerpts establishing how the named scholars evaluate that argument.
   **Material effect:** Could materially strengthen or qualify the assessment of Christian fulfilment, though it cannot make John’s reference explicit.
   **Evidence needed:** The cited Acts passages and the relevant portions of the two scholarly discussions, with precise locators.

4. **Original meaning is underargued in both directions.**
   The critical case reasonably says the psalm *can* describe preservation from death. But that possibility alone does not establish its original meaning. Conversely, the strongest case calls the language “life-after-Sheol” and a “coherent resurrection sequence,” although the supplied verses do not explicitly narrate death followed by restoration. “Eternal pleasures” also deserves consideration when assessing the scope of the speaker’s hope, without assuming it proves resurrection.
   **Material effect:** Could change confidence about original meaning and therefore the “retrospective” classification.
   **Evidence needed:** The complete psalm, analysis of its structure and temporal language, and evidence bearing on the extent of the hope expressed.

5. **Translation choices carry argumentative weight that the publication copy obscures.**
   The packet itself flags a material distinction between Hebrew language that can describe the Pit and Greek wording supporting bodily corruption. Yet the displayed text gives “decay,” while other formulations say “escape from the grave.” Those expressions can encourage different conclusions. Capitalizing “Holy One” also presents an interpretive cue whose significance is not discussed. These are unresolved translation and presentation issues, not demonstrated mistranslations.
   **Material effect:** Potentially material to the original-meaning argument and to explaining Acts’s reading; not to John’s failure to name the passage.
   **Evidence needed:** The relevant Hebrew and Greek wording, translation rationale, and an explanation of how Acts uses the Greek text.

6. **Reception history and interpretive exclusivity are insufficiently separated.**
   “Its specific resurrection meaning comes through Acts’s later argument” may describe where the packet explicitly documents that meaning. It could also imply that Acts originated the interpretation or supplied a meaning absent from every earlier horizon. The packet does not establish either stronger claim. Likewise, “early” is not supported here by a supplied chronology.
   **Material effect:** Could require narrower historical wording; would not overturn the explicit/unnamed distinction.
   **Evidence needed:** Dating evidence and relevant earlier interpretive evidence. Without those, distinguish the earliest evidence supplied from the interpretation’s historical origin.

7. **Duplication is an editorial issue, not evidence against fulfilment.**
   Overlap with claim 070 may justify avoiding double counting. It does not weaken Psalm 16’s interpretive merits. Moreover, claim 070 is absent, so the extent of duplication cannot be independently assessed.
   **Material effect:** Could change whether this entry should stand separately, but not whether Psalm 16 anticipates resurrection.
   **Evidence needed:** Claim 070 and the project’s rules for counting independent claims or evidence.

**Strongest rebuttal to the proposed verdict:** John need not name Psalm 16 for the psalm to be relevant resurrection Scripture. On the packet’s account, Acts explicitly applies it to Jesus twice. A Christian interpreter can argue that these applications disclose a fuller significance consistent with the psalm’s language about bodily security, non-abandonment to Sheol, and enduring divine presence. Showing that preservation from death is a possible original reading does not refute that account.

That rebuttal challenges an exclusive “retrospective rereading” judgment. It does **not** establish that John specifically intended Psalm 16, or that the psalm unambiguously predicts death followed by resurrection. The packet supports the restrained public verdict more securely than its categorical metadata.
