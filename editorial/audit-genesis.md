# Editorial audit: Genesis (entries 1–20)

Status: initial triage, not publication copy.

The audit distinguishes prediction, NT reuse, typology, and loose analogy. “Needs
review” means that the claim raises a material textual or methodological question,
not that it has been rejected without examination.

| ID | Reference | Initial classification | Priority flags |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gen 3:15 | Later messianic reading | “Seed/offspring” is collective; the text does not mention virgin birth; serpent/Satan identification is later |
| 2 | Gen 3:15 | Later messianic reading | Original referents and collective/singular force need treatment; separate from entry 1 |
| 3 | Gen 5:24 | Christian analogy | Enoch is not predicting another ascension; Mark 16:19 belongs to the disputed longer ending |
| 4 | Gen 9:26–27 | Genealogical inference | The source text says “God of Shem,” not “God will become a son of Shem”; Luke supplies ancestry, not fulfilment language |
| 5 | Gen 12:3 | Promise re-read christologically in NT | Gal 3:8 explicitly quotes the promise, but the promise concerns Abraham/all families; distinguish corporate and individual readings |
| 6 | Gen 12:7 | Promise re-read christologically in NT | Gal 3:16’s singular “seed” argument needs linguistic and rhetorical context |
| 7 | Gen 14:18 | NT typology | Hebrews’ priestly claim is mediated principally through Ps 110:4; Gen 14 supplies the character Melchizedek |
| 8 | Gen 14:18 | NT typology and name interpretation | Heb 7:2 explicitly interprets Melchizedek’s name/title; not a prediction made by Genesis |
| 9 | Gen 14:18 | Later Christian typology | No cited NT passage connects Melchizedek’s bread and wine to the Last Supper |
| 10 | Gen 17:19 | Genealogical inference | Rom 9:7 discusses the line of promise/election; verify whether the list’s Jesus-specific framing is representative |
| 11 | Gen 22:8 | Later Christian typology | Genesis supplies a ram, not a lamb; John 1:29 does not cite Genesis 22 |
| 12 | Gen 22:18 | Promise re-read christologically in NT | Gal 3:16 explicitly makes the singular-seed argument; assess collective-noun objection alongside Paul’s rhetoric |
| 13 | Gen 26:2–5 | Unsupported extension of promise | Heb 11:18 concerns Isaac and the promise; “as the Redeemer” is not present in either cited passage |
| 14 | Gen 28:12 | Explicit NT allusion/typology | John 1:51 clearly evokes Jacob’s ladder but substitutes the Son of Man; strong intertext, weak prediction |
| 15 | Gen 28:14 | Genealogical inference | Luke’s genealogy can demonstrate claimed descent but does not identify Gen 28:14 as fulfilled prophecy |
| 16 | Gen 49:10 | Disputed royal/messianic oracle | Hebrew wording and chronology are contested; “time of his coming” overstates what Luke/Galatians establish |
| 17 | Gen 49:10 | Disputed royal/messianic oracle | Judah connection is plausible within Christian genealogy, but not a distinct prediction from entries 16–20 |
| 18 | Gen 49:10 | Translation-dependent interpretation | “Shiloh” and “one sent” are disputed renderings/etymologies; John 17:3 does not cite the passage |
| 19 | Gen 49:10 | Apologetic chronology | “Before Judah lost identity” is absent from the verse; define and source the historical cutoff being claimed |
| 20 | Gen 49:10 | Thematic parallel | John 10:16 does not cite Gen 49:10; “obedience/gathering of peoples” depends partly on textual choice |

## Sources consulted in triage

- [NET Bible notes on Genesis 3:15](https://classic.net.bible.org/verse.php?book=Gen&chapter=3&tab=commentaries&theme=wiki&verse=15) identify “offspring” as a collective singular and document the later messianic reading.
- [NET Bible notes on Genesis 49:10](https://classic.net.bible.org/passage.php?passage=Gen+49%3A10&theme=wiki) outline four major readings of the difficult “Shiloh/to whom it belongs” clause.
- The dataset’s NT references provide the first-pass test for explicit quotation, allusion, or merely thematic comparison. Each will need verification against a critical text and specialist commentary before publication.
