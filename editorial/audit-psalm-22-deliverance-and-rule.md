# Editorial audit: Psalm 22 deliverance and rule

Claims 086–090 complete this run through Psalm 22. They include an explicit Gospel
quotation, two claims that import ideas absent from the cited verses, and two
Christian readings of the psalm's turn from suffering to praise and worldwide rule.

## Decisions

- John explicitly quotes Psalm 22:18 about the garments. This is exceptionally
  close literary fulfilment, but the psalm itself is a lament rather than a
  labelled future oracle.
- Luke's final prayer quotes Psalm 31:5, not Psalm 22:20–21. Psalm 22 contains a
  plea for rescue, not a commitment of the spirit at death.
- Psalm 22:20–21 contains no Satan, Redeemer or bruised heel. Those details have
  been imported from a Christian reading of Genesis 3:15 and Hebrews 2:14.
- Psalm 22:22 follows the psalm's turn toward answered prayer, and Hebrews 2:12
  explicitly gives its words to Jesus. It does not itself state resurrection.
- Psalm 22:27–28 says the LORD rules the nations. Colossians gives Christ cosmic
  status, supporting a Christian identification, but does not cite the psalm or
  turn it into a distinct prediction of a future governor.
