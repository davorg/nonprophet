# Editorial audit: claims 056–065

Claim 056 repeats the Davidic father-son formula already assessed in claims 051
and 055. Claims 059–065 split a single royal psalm into seven propositions;
they are separate social posts, not independent cumulative proofs.

| Claim | Strongest connection | Main audit question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 056 | Hebrews explicitly quotes Chronicles/Samuel about Jesus | Is this new evidence or the same Davidic promise again? |
| 057 | Job wants a mediator; 1 Timothy names Jesus as one | Is a lament about the absence of a mediator a prediction? |
| 058 | Job may hope to see God after death | Does this difficult Hebrew passage teach bodily resurrection, and does it identify Jesus? |
| 059 | Acts explicitly applies Psalm 2's hostile rulers to Jesus | How does later application relate to the original royal setting? |
| 060 | Messiah/Christ literally means “anointed” | Is a common royal title a unique prediction? |
| 061 | Jesus is holy | Does “holy” in Psalm 2:6 describe the king or Mount Zion? |
| 062 | Jesus is called king | Does a generic royal title identify him? |
| 063 | New Testament sonship language draws on Psalm 2 | Does royal adoption equal divine nature? |
| 064 | Acts applies Psalm 2:7 to Jesus's resurrection | Are crucifixion and resurrection actually predicted by the psalm? |
| 065 | Revelation repeatedly applies Psalm 2's iron sceptre to Jesus | Is this explicit reception, direct forecast, or both? |
