# Source standards

Sources support an argument; they do not inherit authority merely by being cited.
Record enough bibliographic detail and a page, section or paragraph locator for
another editor to reproduce every material claim.

## Tiers

1. **Primary and specialist:** biblical manuscripts/editions, peer-reviewed articles,
   academic monographs and technical commentaries by relevant specialists.
2. **Academic synthesis:** university-press reference works, major scholarly
   commentaries and reputable institutional resources that summarize specialist work.
3. **Serious confessional argument:** named scholarly or apologetic authors who state
   the Christian case in a documented, recognizable form.
4. **Popular or discovery-only:** ministry pages, listicles, anonymous sites and search
   snippets. These may locate an argument but should not carry a disputed conclusion.

Tier describes evidential use, not whether a source agrees with us. A Christian
scholar can be Tier 1; a sceptical blog remains Tier 4.

## Minimum evidence per claim

- Cite at least one named source for the strongest Christian case. Prefer Tier 1–3.
- Support material historical, linguistic or textual criticism with Tier 1 or 2.
- Consult the OT passage in context and the cited NT passage directly.
- For translation-dependent claims, record the BSB note and check an appropriate
  Hebrew/Greek critical text or specialist source.
- Never cite a search result, AI response or unsaved snippet as evidence.
- Quote sparingly, exactly and with a locator; use our own words for the argument.

If an accessible strong advocate cannot be found, record the search attempted and
flag the claim. Do not manufacture a stronger defence from unrelated arguments.

Set `checked` only after opening the source and verifying the attributed proposition.
Preserve publication metadata, a stable URL when available, access date for web
sources, and the exact locator. Resolve conflicting claims from the underlying work
rather than counting sources.
