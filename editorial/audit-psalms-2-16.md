# Editorial audit: Psalms 2–16

Claims 066–070 continue the Psalms sequence. The governing distinction is between
what each poem says in its first literary setting and what a New Testament author
later does with it. Explicit quotation is strong evidence for Christian reception;
it is not automatically evidence that the psalm originally described Jesus.

## Decisions

- Treat Psalm 2:12 as textually and translationally difficult. The public argument
  does not depend on resolving *bar* as “son.”
- Treat Matthew 21:16 and Hebrews 2:5–9 as explicit rereadings of Psalm 8, noting
  their dependence on the Septuagint where material.
- Do not manufacture a quotation link between Psalm 9 and Acts 17. Their shared
  judgment language supports a theme, not an identifiable prediction.
- Give Acts 2 and 13 their full evidential weight for Christian use of Psalm 16,
  while distinguishing that use from the psalmist’s original confidence of rescue
  from death.
