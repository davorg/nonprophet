# Editorial audit: claims 031–035

These claims move from a cleansing ritual to the Day of Atonement and the
theology of blood. Hebrews makes the Christian comparisons in claims 032 and
033 explicitly; that still does not turn Leviticus into a set of advance
predictions.

| Claim | Original passage | Strongest Christian connection | Main audit question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 031 | Leviticus 14 returns a healed person to ritual life | Jesus sends a healed man to a priest; Acts later records priestly converts | Does Acts connect those converts with this particular healing? |
| 032 | The high priest enters alone with sacrificial blood | Hebrews contrasts this annual rite with Christ's heavenly, once-for-all offering | Is this an explicit typology or an advance prediction? |
| 033 | Atonement victims are burned outside the camp | Hebrews directly compares this with Jesus suffering outside the gate | Does the geographical parallel predict crucifixion? |
| 034 | Blood is reserved for altar atonement because it bears life | Jesus describes his blood as poured out for forgiveness | Is a general ritual principle a specific forecast? |
| 035 | Blood makes atonement on the altar | First John says Jesus's blood cleanses from sin | Does later Christian reuse establish original predictive intent? |

The two Leviticus 17:11 claims overlap but remain distinct: claim 034 focuses on
blood as life, while claim 035 focuses on its atoning function.
