# Verdict guide

The verdict describes what the evidence supports; it is not a score for Christianity
or for the historical Jesus. Original meaning, later reception, and theological
typology are separate questions.

- `plausible_prediction`: predictive in its original context, sufficiently specific,
  and supported by evidence for the proposed fulfilment.
- `plausible_typology_not_prediction`: a coherent theological correspondence exists,
  but the OT text did not predict the later event in its original setting.
- `retrospective_rereading`: the later text deliberately reapplies or reshapes the OT
  passage; potentially meaningful, but not prior prediction.
- `thematic_parallel_only`: the resemblance is broad, commonplace or unsupported by
  an identifiable quotation, allusion or typological method.
- `depends_on_disputed_text_or_translation`: the claim materially relies on a
  contested manuscript reading or rendering.
- `insufficiently_specific`: the proposal is too general, elastic or post-hoc to
  carry the claimed evidential weight.
- `source_claim_incorrect`: the inherited list misstates a text, reference or alleged
  correspondence.
- `mixed_or_indeterminate`: the evidence points in different directions or the claim
  combines propositions requiring different verdicts.

Use `unassessed` only during production. Confidence measures confidence in the
classification, not confidence about faith. A verdict summary must say what survives
the critique as well as what does not.
