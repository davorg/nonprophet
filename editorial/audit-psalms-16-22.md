# Editorial audit: Psalms 16–22

Claims 071–080 mix explicit Gospel reuse, broad thematic parallels and several
claims that import details absent from their cited psalm verses. The audit treats
the New Testament's use of a psalm as important evidence of Christian reception,
while asking separately whether the psalm stated an identifiable future event.

## Decisions

- Claim 071 substantially duplicates claim 070; its distinctive John citation does
  not identify Psalm 16, though Acts provides the explicit Christian bridge.
- Psalm 17:15 can be read as resurrection hope, waking from sleep, or renewed divine
  encounter. The uncertainty prevents a confident prediction claim.
- Luke deliberately reworks Psalm 18's “horn of salvation” image into a Davidic
  figure, but the psalm originally calls God himself the horn.
- Psalm 22 shaped Matthew's passion narrative strongly. Exact quotation and close
  narrative reuse are acknowledged without turning the original lament into a list
  of independently testable forecasts.
- Claims about three hours of darkness, birth as Saviour and being forsaken because
  of others' sins are assessed against what the cited verses actually say.
