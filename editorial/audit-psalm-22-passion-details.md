# Editorial audit: Psalm 22 passion details

Claims 081–085 move from broad bodily imagery to some of the closest Gospel
reuse of Psalm 22. The audit distinguishes a Gospel writer's literary use of the
psalm from evidence that each line was originally a discrete prediction.

## Decisions

- “Poured out like water” describes the sufferer's collapse; it does not say
  that blood will pour from a pierced side.
- Psalm 22:14–15 and Mark 15 both portray severe suffering, but “agony” is too
  broad to identify a unique predicted event.
- John marks Jesus's thirst as scriptural fulfilment, but does not name the
  scripture. Psalm 22:15 is possible; Psalm 69:21 is a particularly close rival
  because John immediately mentions sour wine.
- Psalm 22:16 has a real and ancient textual problem. The BSB's “pierced” follows
  one early textual tradition; the Masoretic Text reads “like a lion,” while the
  Greek has “dug.” None by itself specifies Roman crucifixion.
- Luke's garments and lots closely reuse Psalm 22:18. This is strong evidence of
  literary application, but the psalm presents a current lament rather than an
  explicitly future oracle.
